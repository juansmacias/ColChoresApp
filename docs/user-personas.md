# User Personas — Family Chores App

**Product:** Family Chores App
**Family profile:** Dad · Mom · Kid (age 10) · Kid (age 3)

---

## Persona 1 — Dad

**Name:** Marcus, 38
**Role in family:** Co-parent, secondary household manager
**Occupation:** Project manager at a mid-size tech company (hybrid, 3 days in office)

### About
Marcus is organized at work but tends to disengage from household management when he comes home. He genuinely wants to help but often doesn't know what needs to be done unless someone tells him. He feels guilty when he realizes his wife has been carrying most of the load, but struggles to stay proactive without a system.

### Goals
- Contribute fairly to the household without needing to be asked
- Be a role model for his kids around responsibility and work ethic
- Reduce friction and arguments about chores on weekday evenings
- Check in on household status when traveling for work

### Frustrations
- "I don't know what needs to be done" — relies on his wife to delegate
- Gets defensive when confronted about unequal contribution because he has no data to counter with
- Finds paper chore charts or whiteboards easy to ignore
- Misses context when he returns from a work trip

### Behaviors
- Checks his phone frequently throughout the day
- Responds well to notifications with clear, actionable tasks
- Prefers a quick overview (dashboard) over detailed lists
- More likely to engage if the app shows his contribution relative to the family

### Quote
> "Just tell me what to do and I'll do it. I don't want to have to figure out what needs doing every night."

### Jobs hired for
- Job 1: Needs to be pulled into coordination, not just pushed tasks
- Job 3: Wants fair-share visibility so he can act before his wife gets frustrated

## Persona 2 — Mom

**Name:** Sofia, 36
**Role in family:** Primary household manager
**Occupation:** Registered nurse, works 3 shifts per week (12-hour days)

### About
Sofia carries the mental load of the household. Even on her days off, she is the one who notices what needs to be done, plans ahead, and makes sure nothing falls through the cracks. She loves her family but is exhausted by the invisible management layer that only she seems to see. She has tried paper charts and group chats — neither stuck.

### Goals
- Delegate household coordination to a system, not to herself
- Ensure her kids are building real habits, not just completing tasks for rewards
- Have a clear record of what was done so she doesn't have to re-inspect everything
- Reduce the emotional labor of reminding and following up

### Frustrations
- Spends mental energy remembering and tracking what everyone else should be doing
- Reminding her husband and kids feels like nagging, which she hates
- Previous systems (whiteboards, sticky notes, group chats) required her to maintain them
- Feels invisible when her own contributions go unacknowledged

### Behaviors
- Primary app user — most likely to set up the system and maintain it
- Will invest time in initial configuration if it saves her time long-term
- Checks the app during breaks at work to see if tasks were completed at home
- Sends reminders through the app rather than texting individually

### Quote
> "I don't want to manage the chores app. I want the chores app to manage the chores."

### Jobs hired for
- Job 1: Primary beneficiary — she is the one being offloaded
- Job 2: Cares deeply about the parenting dimension, not just task completion
- Job 3: Needs acknowledgment of her own contributions, not just visibility into others'

## Persona 3 — Alex (age 10)

**Name:** Alex, 10
**Role in family:** Older sibling
**School:** 4th grade, after-school soccer twice a week

### About
Alex is bright, socially aware, and has a strong sense of fairness. He is old enough to understand responsibility but needs motivation beyond "because I said so." He compares his chore list to his younger sibling's and will call out any perceived unfairness immediately. He responds well to autonomy — he likes being trusted to do things his own way.

### Goals
- Know exactly what is expected of him without having to ask
- Feel like his workload is fair compared to his sister's (adjusted for age)
- Earn tangible rewards (screen time, allowance, or privileges) for completing chores
- Be recognized when he does a good job

### Frustrations
- Being reminded repeatedly — he finds it condescending
- Chores that feel vague ("clean your room" with no clear definition of done)
- Not knowing the "why" behind a task
- His sister getting away with less because she is younger

### Behaviors
- Comfortable with smartphones and tablets
- Engages with gamification (streaks, points, progress bars)
- More likely to complete tasks if he can mark them done himself and see the confirmation
- Motivated by comparison — sibling leaderboard or family contribution view

### Quote
> "Why do I have to do the dishes AND take out the trash? Emma doesn't do anything."

### Jobs hired for
- Job 2: Secondary participant — the system should support his growing autonomy
- Job 3: Fairness visibility is highly relevant to him; he wants proof, not just promises

### Design considerations
- Needs a mobile-first, clean interface — no walls of text
- Task descriptions should include a clear definition of "done" (e.g., a photo check or checklist)
- Rewards must be visible and feel attainable within a short time horizon (days, not months)

## Persona 4 — Emma (age 3)

**Name:** Emma, 3
**Role in family:** Younger sibling
**School:** Attends daycare 4 days per week

### About
Emma cannot read yet and has a very short attention span. She is at the stage where children love to imitate adults and feel proud of "helping." She doesn't understand chores as obligation — she understands them as play and participation. Any engagement with the app is entirely mediated by a parent, but the app can still support her involvement through visual cues and parent-guided interactions.

### Goals
- Feel included in what the family is doing ("I helped!")
- Complete simple, physical tasks with a parent nearby
- Get immediate positive feedback (sound, animation, sticker)

### Frustrations
- Being left out when the rest of the family does something together
- Tasks that require reading, sustained attention, or fine motor precision
- Waiting for delayed rewards — she lives entirely in the present

### Behaviors
- Cannot use the app independently — all interactions are parent-assisted
- Responds to large colorful icons, sounds, and simple animations
- Attention span for a single task: 2–5 minutes maximum
- Motivated entirely by immediate praise and visual celebration

### Quote
*(as told by Mom)*
> "She saw Alex mark a chore done and immediately wanted to press the button too."

### Jobs hired for
- Job 2: Her "chores" are developmentally appropriate contributions — putting toys in a bin, carrying her plate to the sink, feeding the pet with supervision. The goal is habit formation and belonging, not productivity.

### Design considerations
- Emma's tasks should be a separate, simplified view (large icons, picture-based, no text)
- Completion should trigger an immediate celebration animation — instant reward loop
- A parent must be able to "complete on behalf of" Emma with a single tap
- Emma's data should not be included in fairness comparisons — her participation is developmental, not transactional
